Shield

Privacy Policy

Shield blocks gambling on your phone, at the DNS layer, before a betting site can load. This page explains exactly what the app stores, what it sends, and what it never touches.

  • No ads
  • No analytics
  • No crash reporters
  • No tracking
  • AES-256 backups
Effective 26 September 2026 · Applies to Shield 1.0.0 and later

Read this part first. Shield is not a completely offline app, and this policy will not pretend otherwise. Two things cross the network:

1. Blocklist updates. The app downloads a signed list of gambling domains over HTTPS. That request contains nothing about you. A local seed list ships inside the app, so blocking works with no network at all.

2. Accountability pairing, only if you turn it on. It is off by default. If you pair with a parent, partner or sponsor, the app sends a pseudonymous device ID, your app version, whether protection is on, and blocked-attempt events. Nothing else.

Everything else — your streak, journal, cravings, milestones, Fortress schedules, Guardian PIN and encrypted backups — never leaves your device.

1. What Shield never does

  • ✓No advertising SDKs, and no ads in any form.
  • ✓No analytics, telemetry or usage-measurement SDK.
  • ✓No third-party crash reporter. Crashes are not sent to us.
  • ✓No advertising ID, no cross-app identifier, no device fingerprinting.
  • ✓No data broker, no attribution SDK, no session recording.
  • ✓No background upload of your journal, notes or streak.
  • ✓No selling or sharing of your data for money.

This is verifiable rather than a promise. Every dependency in the app is Jetpack, AndroidX, kotlinx, or written by us. There is no Firebase, no Crashlytics, no ad network and no attribution library in the dependency graph, and the source is public.

2. How the blocking works, and why your browsing stays on your phone

Shield uses Android's VpnService in a local, loopback configuration. That sounds alarming, so here is the precise version:

  • Android asks every app on the device to route its traffic through one network interface. Shield becomes that interface, only on this device.
  • When another app looks up a domain, the DNS question arrives at Shield on the device. Shield answers it from its own list, or refuses it.
  • Your web traffic itself is not sent to us and not inspected. We handle the name lookup. The connection, if it is allowed at all, goes straight from your app to the destination.

So no browsing history is assembled, stored or transmitted. There is no server that could be asked what you visited, because there is no server involved. No DNS query, hostname, page visited, or traffic payload is ever uploaded.

3. What is stored on your device

Everything below stays in the app's private storage.
DataWhere it livesWhy
Clean streak, start date, money-saved estimate On-device database / preferences To show your progress. Self-declared spend, never transmitted.
Craving journal and triggers On-device database (Room / SQLite) So you can spot patterns. Never uploaded.
Custom exceptions and blocklist On-device database Your own allow/deny decisions. Local only.
Fortress window schedules On-device database To lock down during your own high-risk hours.
Guardian PIN On-device, as a salted PBKDF2 hash To gate your own settings changes. Never included in a backup.
Encrypted backup file Only where you choose, via the system file picker Sealed with AES-256-GCM. The key comes from your passphrase, which is never stored.

Backups are encrypted before they touch storage using AES-256-GCM, with the key derived from your passphrase by PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256 at 120,000 iterations and a random per-file salt. Every export uses a fresh nonce, and any modification to the file makes it fail to decrypt rather than decrypt to something wrong. This means that if you lose the passphrase, the file cannot be opened by anyone — including us. There is no recovery key, no escrow and no back door.

Shield never picks a file path on its own. The app can only read or write the single file you explicitly select through Android's system file picker, and you can revoke that access from system settings at any time.

One setting changes the answer. Shield has an “exact domain sharing” option that, when you enable it, tells your paired support person which specific site was blocked. It is off by default and blocked domain names are not sent unless you turn it on. If you enable it, the domain names you blocked become shared browsing data, and this policy says so plainly.

This data is used for the feature you asked for. It is not used for advertising, and not used for tracking in the sense Google Play defines it: it is never shared with third parties for ad or measurement purposes, and it is not linked across apps.

5. Who can see your data

  • Nobody else. Your recovery data is not visible to us, to other Shield users, or to anyone with access to your unlocked phone.
  • Your paired support person sees only what you chose to share when you set up Accountability, and only while the pairing exists.
  • We do not sell, rent or trade data. There are no third-party analytics or advertising partners to share it with.
  • Legal process. We do not hold your identity, so we cannot voluntarily hand over information about a specific person. Valid legal requests are handled through the contact address below.

6. How long we keep things

  • On-device data stays until you delete it, clear it in the app, or uninstall.
  • Accountability device and pairing records are kept until you unpair or ask us to delete them.
  • Session tokens expire on a set lifetime. Administrative sessions expire too.
  • Nothing is kept for advertising or analytics, because nothing is collected for those purposes.

7. Your choices

  • Turn the VPN off, or stop Shield, at any time from the app.
  • Unpair to remove the pairing and its stored token from your device immediately.
  • Export a backup whenever you like, and delete it whenever you like.
  • Ask us to delete your accountability data at any time, using the address below.
  • Uninstall. On-device data is removed with the app.

8. Security

  • All network traffic uses HTTPS. The app does not permit cleartext traffic.
  • Blocklist updates are cryptographically signed, so a tampered list is rejected rather than trusted.
  • Backups use AES-256-GCM with a per-file random salt and a fresh nonce per export.
  • The Guardian PIN is stored as a salted, iterated PBKDF2 hash, never in plain text, and never in a backup.

9. Children's privacy

Shield is a recovery tool for adults aged 18 and over. It is not directed at children and is not marketed to them. The Accountability feature is designed for an adult in recovery choosing to involve a trusted adult; it is not a parental-control product. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. If you believe a child has provided us information, contact us and we will delete it.

10. Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to access, correct, export or delete your personal data, or to object to its processing. Because almost all of your data never leaves your device, you can exercise most of these rights yourself in the app without contacting us at all. For anything held on our servers, email us and we will action it.

11. Changes to this policy

If this policy changes materially, the new effective date at the top of the page changes with it, and the change is noted in the app's release notes on Google Play. Continuing to use Shield after a change means the updated policy applies.

13. A note on the "no tracking" claim

Plenty of apps say this and mean "we do not sell your data". Here is the narrower, more useful version: if you never open Accountability, Shield makes no request that contains anything about you, ever. It fetches a static, signed list of domains and that is the whole conversation. You can read the source and check.
